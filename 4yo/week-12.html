Week 12

Week 12 is consolidation and celebration. The focus across all five domains is recognising how far each child and parent has come since Week 1 — the step-hop of an early skip attempt, the first because sentence, the first rhyme spotted, the first peer name learned, the first lunchbox opened alone — rather than introducing anything new. The final session closes with a warm, consistent farewell and a practical conversation about the transition to school.

Gross Motor Pod
Consolidation — A Full Skills Showcase
FMS: All Term Skills
▾
Why This Matters

This final motor session brings together everything practised across the term — the step-hop of skipping, throwing and catching, stairs, hopping, balance, and the fine-motor pre-writing marks — as a celebration circuit rather than a teaching session. The goal is to notice growth from Week 1, not to introduce anything new.

Activities
  • Skills showcase circuit (EYLF 3): Every station from the term, set up together, moved through at each child’s own pace.
  • Personal best balance attempt (EYLF 3): One final, celebrated attempt at each child’s hardest balance variant.
  • Draw a person — final version (EYLF 4): Compare, gently and privately, to the Week 8 attempt — most children will show real growth in the number of body parts included.

VariationCompare each child only to their own Week 1 self this week — the growth across 12 weeks is the whole point, not comparison between children.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

Let children choose their favourite station to finish on — ending the term on a skill they feel confident in matters more than covering every station evenly.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

When a parent asks how their child compares to others: "The most meaningful comparison is to their own Week 1 — and that growth has been real and steady."

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: over 12 weeks, most children in this program have visibly grown more willing to attempt hard physical skills in front of others — that shift in confidence is the deeper achievement underneath every specific skill.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Celebrate Growth, Not Mastery

Parents name one specific thing their child couldn't do in Week 1 that they can do now.

🏠 Try This at Home

Try the whole obstacle course together at a park — skipping, throwing, catching, balance. Notice out loud what's changed since the term began.

Communication Pod
Vocabulary and Sentence Celebration
▾
Why This Matters

By the end of this program, most children will be using longer, more connected sentences with reasons (“because”), comparisons, and time language than they were in Week 1. This final week celebrates that growth from each child's own starting point, and reviews personal information — name, age, and family details — as a genuinely useful, practical closing activity.

🦉 OWL Reminder — Final Week

Reflect on your own OWL practice this term — Observe, Wait, Listen. Which came easiest? Which took the most conscious effort? Share with the group if you would like.

Why OWL? Evidence & references →

Activities
  • Personal information review (EYLF 5): A relaxed circle-time game — first name, last name, age, and (if ready) a phone number or address, celebrated as new knowledge rather than tested.
  • Sentence-length celebration (EYLF 5): Compare a Week 1 sentence sample to one from this week, if recordings or notes exist.
  • Group storytelling (EYLF 5): Build one shared story together, each child adding a because or a describing word.

VariationIf a child forgets their address under the pressure of a group setting, gently move on — this is exactly the kind of detail better practised naturally at home over time.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

Personal information like a full address or phone number is genuinely hard to hold — celebrate whatever a child can offer rather than expecting the full list.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

When a parent is surprised by how much language has grown: "That's 12 weeks of consistent everyday practice — the same conversations you'll keep having long after the program ends."

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: offering a full sentence, a personal fact, or a piece of reasoning in front of the group is a confident act by the end of this term — many children arrived far more hesitant to speak up in Week 1.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Celebrate Growth, Not Milestones

Parents name one specific way their child's talking has changed since Week 1.

🏠 Try This at Home

Practise saying full name, age, and address together as a fun daily habit — in the car, on a walk. Keep a simple word book of new things your child can say.

Cognitive Pod
Routine Mastery and Term Review
EF: Multi-Step Routine, Independent
▾
Why This Matters

Completing a familiar multi-step routine — pack up, wash hands, come to the group — without individual prompting is the clearest, most practical evidence of everything this term's pre-literacy, numeracy, and executive-function threads have built toward. This week also offers a light, celebratory review of the alphabet and number threads from across the term.

Activities
  • Full routine without individual prompts (EYLF 3): Use only a group song or signal — notice who completes the sequence independently.
  • Alphabet and numeral celebration (EYLF 5): A relaxed group game reviewing letters and numbers covered this term — not a test, a celebration of what's now familiar.
  • Rhyme and pattern recall (EYLF 5): Revisit a favourite rhyme or pattern activity from earlier in the term.

VariationKeep the alphabet/numeral review genuinely playful — a quiz-like tone undoes the confidence-building work of the rest of the term.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

If a child still needs individual prompting for the routine, that's useful information for continued home practice — not a concern to raise as a failure at the final session.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

On the term as a whole: "What you're seeing today is 12 weeks of steady, everyday practice — not anything that happened in one session."

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: independently completing a known routine, without being told each step, is a concrete, visible marker of a child's growing initiative — genuinely worth naming out loud to the child, not just to the parent.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Celebrate Routine Mastery

Parents notice how much executive function their child is showing in everyday routines now, and commit to keeping those routines going at home.

🏠 Try This at Home

Run one familiar home routine using only a song or signal, no verbal instructions. Notice what your child can now do independently that they couldn't manage alone in Week 1.

Social-Emotional Pod
Peer Recognition and Community Farewell
SEL: Social Awareness
▾
Why This Matters

A well-managed ending teaches children that goodbyes are safe and that community continues beyond one setting. Naming every peer by name, reflecting on the term's friendships, and sharing a consistent farewell ritual consolidates the social-emotional growth — from parallel play in Week 1 to leading a game for peers in Week 11 — that this whole term has tracked.

Activities
  • Name every peer (EYLF 1): Who's here today? — by the final week, most children can name the whole group.
  • Reflecting on friendships (EYLF 2): A simple, warm conversation — who did you like playing with this term? What did you play?
  • Group farewell ritual (EYLF 1): The same closing song, wave, or gesture used consistently, giving the ending a predictable, comforting shape.

VariationIf a child seems subdued about the ending, that's a normal response to a genuine change — naming it warmly (“it's the last day, and that can feel a bit sad”) is more helpful than rushing past it.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

Keep the farewell ritual identical to whatever's been used before, if anything has — consistency matters more at an ending than novelty.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

On the group's growth as a whole: "This group went from barely knowing each other's names to genuinely playing and leading games together — that's a real community, built in 12 weeks."

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: this term has tracked children's growing willingness to initiate — using a peer's name, inviting someone into play, leading a game. A warm, well-marked ending protects that confidence going forward, rather than letting it dissipate with an abrupt stop.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Celebrate the Parent's Journey Too

The facilitator names what parents themselves have learned and practised over the term — the coaching focus each week wasn't only for the child.

🏠 Try This at Home

Talk about the Play Pods friends at home after the program ends — look at any photos, ask about them. The friendships and the community don't have to end when the sessions do.

Self-Help Pod
Independence Celebration and School-Readiness Send-Off
ADL: All Categories
▾
Why This Matters

This final self-help session brings together dressing, hygiene, mealtime, and routine independence into one celebratory review, and closes with a direct conversation about what changes as children move toward school — the same independence built here is exactly what a classroom will expect.

Activities
  • Independence showcase (EYLF 3): A relaxed, unhurried run-through of the skills built this term — shoes, lunchbox, dressing, handwashing — noticing growth rather than testing performance.
  • What will school need? conversation (EYLF 3): A simple, reassuring conversation with parents about the self-help expectations of a typical classroom, linked directly to what's been practised.

VariationIf a skill still needs support, frame it as a clear, specific focus for the remaining months before school starts, rather than a gap to worry about now.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

Resist turning this into a formal assessment — the tone should be closer to a proud show-and-tell than a checklist.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

On school transition: "Everything practised this term — opening containers, managing a jacket, using the toilet independently — is exactly what a classroom expects on day one."

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: I can do it myself has been the throughline of this entire domain across the term — by Week 12, most children carry that stance into new, unfamiliar tasks as well, which is the real transfer this program has been building toward.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Celebrate What's Changed Since Week 1

Parents name one specific self-help skill their child has gained independence in since the term began.

🏠 Try This at Home

Keep practising the routines that still need support — the program ends, but daily life keeps offering the same practice opportunities every day.
