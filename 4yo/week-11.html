Week 11

Week 11 builds on the term's progression: obstacle course integrating the term's skills in motor, 5+ word sentences with connectors in communication, simple addition and subtraction with objects in cognitive development, leading a game for peers in social-emotional growth, and full morning routine independence in self-help. This week also continues the balance, locomotor warm-up, and letter/numeral threads running across the whole term.

Stand on the either foot for personal best attempt.

🔄 Locomotor Warm-Up

All variations combined into one circuit.

Activities
  • Full obstacle circuit (EYLF 3): Stations for each major skill practised this term, moved through at the child's own pace.
  • Jumping backward — building to 5+ (EYLF 3): A final push on the backward-jumping skill introduced in Week 6.
  • Balance challenge — personal best (EYLF 3): One last attempt at each child's hardest balance variant.
  • Locomotor warm-up (EYLF 3): All variations combined into one circuit.

VariationVary the activity to keep engagement high while practising the same underlying skill.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

Let children choose which station to repeat if time allows — returning to a favourite or a recently-improved skill reinforces confidence going into the final week.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

When a parent worries about pace: "Every child develops these skills at a different rate — what matters is regular, playful practice."

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: attempting a genuinely hard physical skill in front of peers, even imperfectly, is an act of initiative worth naming and celebrating.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Effort Over Mastery

Parents celebrate the attempt at a hard skill, not just a perfect result.

Why This Matters

Producing sentences of five or more words using connecting words (and, but, so) is a genuine grammatical complexity milestone — the culmination of the term's steady progression from simple sentences to complex, connected reasoning.

Activities
  • Extended sentence practice (EYLF 5): Encourage children to combine two ideas with "and," "but," or "so" when describing their play.
  • Spatial concept — right/left (EYLF 5): Right and left of self first, then extending to right and left of another person — genuinely harder.

VariationBring the target language into an existing favourite activity rather than treating it as a stand-alone drill.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

Right/left of others is a notably harder concept than right/left of self — most 4-year-olds are still developing this, so treat any correct answer as a genuine achievement.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

When a parent answers for the child: "Try waiting five seconds before stepping in — the pause does more work than a prompt."

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: offering language, even imperfectly, is a small act of communicative initiative — respond warmly to the attempt.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Model, Then Wait

Parents offer the language model, then give real waiting time before jumping in.

Cognitive Pod
Simple Addition and Subtraction with Objects
Pre-Literacy: Alphabet recitation — full
▾
Why This Matters

Using real objects and pictures to solve simple addition and subtraction word problems — rather than abstract number facts — matches how young children genuinely reason about quantity, and this week's word problems (pictures + oral solving) reflect that concrete approach directly.

🧠 Pre-Literacy / Numeracy Threads This Week

Pre-Literacy: Alphabet recitation — full + Numeracy: Word problems — subtraction (with pictures)

Activities
  • Picture word problems — addition (EYLF 4): "There are 3 rabbits, 2 more hop over — how many now?" — solved with real objects, not written sums.
  • Picture word problems — subtraction (EYLF 4): The same approach, taking objects away rather than adding.
  • Alphabet recitation — full (EYLF 5): Reciting the alphabet said, not sung, as a final check-in on this term's letter thread.

VariationTurn the core activity into a short game with a silly or novel twist to keep motivation high.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

Always let the child count out and physically move the objects themselves — solving the problem by counting is exactly the right strategy at this age, not a shortcut to move past.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

On persistence: if a child loses interest partway through a tricky cognitive task, invite one more playful attempt rather than letting the first try be the only one.

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: guessing or attempting an answer in front of others carries a small social risk — a relaxed tone protects a child's willingness to keep trying.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Support the Thinking, Not the Answer

Parents make the task slightly easier when needed, rather than supplying the answer directly.

🏠 Try This at Home

"There are 3 apples, 2 more get added — how many now?" Let them count it out themselves.

Social-Emotional Pod
Leading a Game for Peers
SEL: Initiative
▾
Why This Matters

Taking the lead role in a group game — explaining the rules, choosing who goes first — is a genuine act of initiative, and a fitting capstone to a term that has tracked the shift from Autonomy toward Initiative all along.

Activities
  • Child-led game (EYLF 2): With light facilitator support, let a child choose and explain a simple game to the group.
  • Rotating leadership (EYLF 2): Give each child a turn at a small leadership role across the session — leading a song, choosing the next activity.

VariationLook for a natural moment during free play where this week's skill genuinely comes up, rather than only practising it in a set activity.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

Support quietly from the side rather than taking over if a child's explanation is unclear — let the group ask their own clarifying questions first.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

Model the target behaviour yourself first — children copy what they see far more readily than what they're told.

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: this term has tracked a steady shift from needing support (Autonomy) toward attempting things independently in a social context (Initiative) — this week's skill sits squarely in that shift.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Notice and Name It

Parents notice and warmly name the target behaviour when it happens, rather than only correcting when it doesn't.

🏠 Try This at Home

Choose a simple game and let them explain the rules to the family.

Self-Help Pod
Full Morning Routine Independence
ADL: Routine Independence
▾
Why This Matters

Bringing together dressing, hygiene, and bag-packing into one continuous, largely self-directed morning routine is the genuine self-help capstone of the term — not a new skill, but the integration of everything already built.

Activities
  • Full routine walkthrough (EYLF 3): Dressing, handwashing, and bag-packing in sequence, with the facilitator observing rather than directing.
  • Self-check against a visual list (EYLF 3): The child checks their own readiness against a picture checklist before moving on.

VariationBuild the practice into a real daily routine rather than a stand-alone drill — the transfer to real life matters most.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

Resist stepping in unless genuinely asked — this week is about seeing how much of the routine has become independent, not teaching anything new.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

When a parent steps in too quickly: "Give it a little longer before helping — the attempt itself is the practice."

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: "I can do it myself" is the developmental hallmark of this stage — even when it's slower or messier than an adult stepping in.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Let Them Struggle Before You Help

Parents wait through a genuine struggle before stepping in, rather than helping out of habit.

🏠 Try This at Home

Dressing, hygiene, and bag-packing in one go, with you observing rather than directing.
