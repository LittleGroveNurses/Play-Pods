Week 2

Week 2 builds on the term's progression: throwing for accuracy in motor, asking and answering 'why' in communication, sequencing a plan in cognitive development, cooperative play toward a shared goal in social-emotional growth, and full independent handwashing in self-help. This week also continues the balance, locomotor warm-up, and letter/numeral threads running across the whole term.

Gross Motor Pod
Throwing for Accuracy
FMS: Overhand Throw — Accuracy
▾
Why This Matters

Building on the general overarm throw introduced at 2 and 3, 4-year-olds are ready to work on accuracy — throwing toward a specific target rather than just for distance. This requires the child to adjust force and aim, a genuinely harder coordination task than throwing alone.

🏃 FMS Technique Cues — Throwing for Accuracy
Step forward with the opposite foot to the throwing arm
Turn your side toward the target before you throw
Aim, then release — don't rush the throw
Follow through with your arm after release

FMS evidence & branding note →

⚖️ Balance Challenge This Week

Stand on the preferred foot for 1 second.

🔄 Locomotor Warm-Up

Tiptoe walking, 3 steps.

Activities
  • Target throw (EYLF 3): Beanbags or soft balls thrown at a hoop, basket, or taped target on a wall, starting close and moving back gradually.
  • Step-and-throw drill (EYLF 3): Practise the opposite-foot step in isolation before adding the throw.
  • Balance challenge (EYLF 3): Stand on the preferred foot for 1 second.
  • Locomotor warm-up (EYLF 3): Tiptoe walking, 3 steps.

VariationVary the activity to keep engagement high while practising the same underlying skill.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

Start with a large, close target and a light beanbag — success early on builds willingness to keep practising. Move the target further away only once the step-and-turn technique is consistent.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

When a parent worries about pace: "Every child develops these skills at a different rate — what matters is regular, playful practice."

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: attempting a genuinely hard physical skill in front of peers, even imperfectly, is an act of initiative worth naming and celebrating.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Effort Over Mastery

Parents celebrate the attempt at a hard skill, not just a perfect result.

🏠 Try This at Home

Step forward with the opposite foot to your throwing arm before you let go. Aim at a basket or box.

Communication Pod
Asking and Answering 'Why'
▾
Why This Matters

Following last week's introduction to "because," this week flips the direction — children practise generating their own why-questions, not just answering them. Curious why-questions are a hallmark of 4-year-old reasoning, and responding to them warmly (even the repetitive ones) keeps that curiosity alive.

📚 Book of the Week

The Rainbow Fish — Marcus Pfister

A fish learns that sharing brings more joy than hoarding — a natural pairing for this week's cooperative-play focus.

More book strategies at Hanen Book Nook →

Activities
  • Why-question chain (EYLF 5): Take turns asking and answering why-questions about a picture or story.
  • Spatial concept — in/out (EYLF 5): Practise placing objects in and out of containers, naming the concept each time.
  • Object use — car, bed, chair (EYLF 5): "What is a car for?" — practise explaining the function of everyday objects.

VariationBring the target language into an existing favourite activity rather than treating it as a stand-alone drill.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

If a child's why-questions become repetitive or off-topic, that's developmentally normal — answer briefly and warmly rather than shutting the questioning down.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

When a parent answers for the child: "Try waiting five seconds before stepping in — the pause does more work than a prompt."

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: offering language, even imperfectly, is a small act of communicative initiative — respond warmly to the attempt.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Model, Then Wait

Parents offer the language model, then give real waiting time before jumping in.

🏠 Try This at Home

"Why do we wear a coat?" Give five seconds before helping with the answer.

Cognitive Pod
Sequencing a Plan
Pre-Literacy: Point to 5 uppercase letters
▾
Why This Matters

Being able to describe a simple plan in order — first this, then that — is an early executive-function skill that supports both storytelling and following multi-step routines later. This week also continues the letter-recognition and numeral-reading threads.

🧠 Pre-Literacy / Numeracy Threads This Week

Pre-Literacy: Point to 5 uppercase letters + Numeracy: Reading numerals to 10

Activities
  • "First... then..." planning (EYLF 4): Before an activity, ask the child to say what they'll do first and what comes next.
  • Pre-literacy — Point to 5 uppercase letters (EYLF 5).
  • Numeracy — Reading numerals to 10 (EYLF 4).

VariationTurn the core activity into a short game with a silly or novel twist to keep motivation high.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

Model the frame first — "first I'll get the blocks, then I'll build a tower" — before asking the child to generate their own plan.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

On persistence: if a child loses interest partway through a tricky cognitive task, invite one more playful attempt rather than letting the first try be the only one.

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: guessing or attempting an answer in front of others carries a small social risk — a relaxed tone protects a child's willingness to keep trying.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Support the Thinking, Not the Answer

Parents make the task slightly easier when needed, rather than supplying the answer directly.

🏠 Try This at Home

Before an activity, say what you'll do first and what comes next — model it if they're stuck.

Social-Emotional Pod
Cooperative Play Toward a Shared Goal
SEL: Relationship Skills
▾
Why This Matters

This is the shift from associative play (playing alongside peers with the same materials) to true cooperative play (working together toward one outcome) — a genuine developmental leap for 4-year-olds. A shared building or collecting goal gives children a concrete reason to coordinate with a peer rather than just tolerate them.

Activities
  • Build it together (EYLF 2): Two children build one tower or structure with a shared goal, not just parallel towers.
  • Collecting challenge (EYLF 2): Partners collect a set number of objects together, encouraging them to divide the task.
  • Preferred playmate pairing: Where possible, let children choose or be paired with a child they've shown a preference for — this is itself a developmental milestone worth supporting.

VariationLook for a natural moment during free play where this week's skill genuinely comes up, rather than only practising it in a set activity.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

If two children default to parallel play even with shared materials, name the shared goal explicitly: "you're both building one big tower together!"

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

Model the target behaviour yourself first — children copy what they see far more readily than what they're told.

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: this term has tracked a steady shift from needing support (Autonomy) toward attempting things independently in a social context (Initiative) — this week's skill sits squarely in that shift.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Notice and Name It

Parents notice and warmly name the target behaviour when it happens, rather than only correcting when it doesn't.

🏠 Try This at Home

Pick one shared goal, like one big tower, rather than two separate ones.

Self-Help Pod
Full Independent Handwashing
ADL: Hygiene Routine
▾
Why This Matters

Handwashing is a routine 4-year-olds are expected to manage independently before school — turning on the tap, using soap, and drying hands without step-by-step prompting.

Activities
  • Independent handwashing (EYLF 3): Practise the full sequence at every transition this week, prompting only if a step is missed.
  • Visual routine card (EYLF 3): A simple picture sequence by the sink as a memory support.

VariationBuild the practice into a real daily routine rather than a stand-alone drill — the transfer to real life matters most.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

If a child skips soap or drying, point to the visual card rather than telling them directly — this builds the habit of self-checking against a reference.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

When a parent steps in too quickly: "Give it a little longer before helping — the attempt itself is the practice."

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: "I can do it myself" is the developmental hallmark of this stage — even when it's slower or messier than an adult stepping in.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Let Them Struggle Before You Help

Parents wait through a genuine struggle before stepping in, rather than helping out of habit.

🏠 Try This at Home

Let them do every step themselves — tap, soap, rinse, dry — even if it's slow.
