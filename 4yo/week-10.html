Week 10

This week also continues the balance, locomotor warm-up, and letter/numeral threads running across the whole term.

Ball Skills — Dribbling and Catching a Smaller Ball
FMS: Dribbling + Small-Ball Catch
▾
Why This Matters

Dribbling (bouncing and controlling a ball while moving) is a complex, continuous coordination task, and catching a smaller ball (like a tennis ball, rather than a large playground ball) is a genuine step up in the precision the earlier catching practice built toward.

🏃 FMS Technique Cues — Ball Skills — Dribbling and Catching a Smaller Ball
Push the ball down, don't slap it
Fingertips, not palm, on the ball
For small-ball catching: track it all the way into your hands
Both hands ready, before it arrives

FMS evidence & branding note →

⚖️ Balance Challenge This Week

Stand on the either foot, child's choice for personal best so far.

🔄 Locomotor Warm-Up

Heel-to-toe forward, 5 steps (consolidating).

Activities
  • Dribbling practice (EYLF 3): Bounce and catch first, then bounce while walking a short distance.
  • Small-ball catching (EYLF 3): A tennis ball or similar, thrown gently and at a short distance, building on the larger-ball catching already established.
  • Balance challenge (EYLF 3): Balance review — child's choice of the hardest variant attempted so far.
  • Locomotor warm-up (EYLF 3): Heel-to-toe forward, 5 steps (consolidating).

VariationVary the activity to keep engagement high while practising the same underlying skill.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

Dribbling while stationary should be solid before adding walking — trying to combine both from the start usually leads to frustration with neither skill improving.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

When a parent worries about pace: "Every child develops these skills at a different rate — what matters is regular, playful practice."

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: attempting a genuinely hard physical skill in front of peers, even imperfectly, is an act of initiative worth naming and celebrating.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Effort Over Mastery

Parents celebrate the attempt at a hard skill, not just a perfect result.

🏠 Try This at Home

Bounce and catch first, then try bouncing while walking a short distance.

Answering 'How' and Predicting Outcomes
▾
Why This Matters

"How" questions require a different kind of reasoning than "what" or "why" — they ask for a process or mechanism, and predicting what happens next in a story builds the same inferential thinking this week's mystery-egg book supports directly.

📚 Book of the Week

The Odd Egg — Emily Gravett

A mystery egg invites prediction — directly supports this week's 'what do you think will happen?' language.

More book strategies at Hanen Book Nook →

Activities
  • "How" question practice (EYLF 5): "How do we make toast?" — process-based questions distinct from simple why-questions.
  • Predicting outcomes (EYLF 5): Pause this week's book before a reveal and ask "what do you think will happen?"
  • Spatial concept — above/below, centre/corner (EYLF 5): Positioning practice with these paired concepts.
  • Object use consolidation (EYLF 5): A full review of all 12 objects covered this term.

VariationBring the target language into an existing favourite activity rather than treating it as a stand-alone drill.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

Accept any plausible prediction warmly, even an incorrect one — the reasoning process matters more than guessing the right outcome.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

When a parent answers for the child: "Try waiting five seconds before stepping in — the pause does more work than a prompt."

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: offering language, even imperfectly, is a small act of communicative initiative — respond warmly to the attempt.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Model, Then Wait

Parents offer the language model, then give real waiting time before jumping in.

🏠 Try This at Home

"How do we make toast?" — a different kind of question to why or what.

Beginning Letter-Sound Matching
Pre-Literacy: Name the remaining letters — full review
▾
Why This Matters

Matching a letter to its sound (not just its name) is the genuine bridge into early phonics — a distinct skill from letter naming, which the term has focused on until now.

🧠 Pre-Literacy / Numeracy Threads This Week

Pre-Literacy: Name the remaining letters — full review + Numeracy: Word problems — addition (with pictures)

Activities
  • Letter-sound matching (EYLF 5): "S says sss — what starts with that sound?" starting with the child's own name letter.
  • Pre-literacy — Name the remaining letters — full review (EYLF 5).
  • Numeracy — Word problems — addition (with pictures) (EYLF 4).

VariationTurn the core activity into a short game with a silly or novel twist to keep motivation high.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

Letter name and letter sound are genuinely separate pieces of knowledge — a child who confidently names a letter may not yet know its sound, and that's an expected, normal gap at this age.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

On persistence: if a child loses interest partway through a tricky cognitive task, invite one more playful attempt rather than letting the first try be the only one.

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: guessing or attempting an answer in front of others carries a small social risk — a relaxed tone protects a child's willingness to keep trying.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Support the Thinking, Not the Answer

Parents make the task slightly easier when needed, rather than supplying the answer directly.

🏠 Try This at Home

Pause before a page turn and ask what they think will happen.

Social-Emotional Pod
Including a Peer Who's Left Out
SEL: Relationship Skills
▾
Why This Matters

Noticing when a peer is on the outside of a group activity and actively inviting them in is a mature prosocial skill — a natural extension of this term's growing empathy and cooperative-play focus.

Activities
  • Noticing exclusion (EYLF 2): Gently point out a child playing alone and wonder aloud together what invitation might look like.
  • Practised invitations (EYLF 2): Model simple inclusive language — "do you want to play with us?" — and practise it directly.

VariationLook for a natural moment during free play where this week's skill genuinely comes up, rather than only practising it in a set activity.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

Facilitators can seed the opportunity deliberately — briefly separate one child during free play so there's a genuine, low-stakes moment to notice and include them.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

Model the target behaviour yourself first — children copy what they see far more readily than what they're told.

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: this term has tracked a steady shift from needing support (Autonomy) toward attempting things independently in a social context (Initiative) — this week's skill sits squarely in that shift.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Notice and Name It

Parents notice and warmly name the target behaviour when it happens, rather than only correcting when it doesn't.

🏠 Try This at Home

Gently wonder together what an invitation to play might sound like.

Self-Help Pod
Independent Toilet Hygiene, No Prompts
ADL: Toileting Independence
▾
Why This Matters

This week explicitly targets the top tier of the toileting progression — the child recognising and acting on the need without being asked, and managing the full hygiene routine unprompted.

Activities
  • Unprompted routine (EYLF 3): Observe and support without initiating — let the child lead the whole sequence this week.
  • Positive reinforcement (EYLF 3): Notice and name independent initiation warmly when it happens, without making a big production of it.

VariationBuild the practice into a real daily routine rather than a stand-alone drill — the transfer to real life matters most.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

If a child still needs occasional prompting, that's fine — note it as a target for continued home practice rather than a concern at this stage.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

When a parent steps in too quickly: "Give it a little longer before helping — the attempt itself is the practice."

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: "I can do it myself" is the developmental hallmark of this stage — even when it's slower or messier than an adult stepping in.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Let Them Struggle Before You Help

Parents wait through a genuine struggle before stepping in, rather than helping out of habit.

🏠 Try This at Home

Step back and let them lead the whole sequence this week.
