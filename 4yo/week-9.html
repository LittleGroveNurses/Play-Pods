Week 9

Week 9 builds on the term's progression: galloping and skipping refinement in motor, time-concept language in communication, complex pretend play, multiple roles in cognitive development, recognising a wider range of emotions in others in social-emotional growth, and packing own bag / visual routine in self-help. This week also continues the balance, locomotor warm-up, and letter/numeral threads running across the whole term.

Galloping and Skipping Refinement
FMS: Gallop / Skip Refinement
▾
Why This Matters

Returning to the skip/gallop introduced in Week 1, this week refines technique — smoothing the rhythm, encouraging longer sequences of consecutive steps, and for some children, the first true alternating-foot skip.

🏃 FMS Technique Cues — Galloping and Skipping Refinement
Keep the rhythm steady — don't rush
Try to get five consecutive steps without stopping
If you're galloping, that's still great progress
Arms swing to help the rhythm

⚖️ Balance Challenge This Week

Stand on the other foot for 1 second, eyes closed.

🔄 Locomotor Warm-Up

Tiptoe walking, 4 steps (consolidating).

Activities
  • Gallop/skip refinement (EYLF 3): Longer practice sequences than Week 1, aiming for consecutive steps rather than single attempts.
  • Music-paced practice (EYLF 3): A steady beat to help find and hold the rhythm.
  • Balance challenge (EYLF 3): Stand on the other foot for 1 second, eyes closed.
  • Locomotor warm-up (EYLF 3): Tiptoe walking, 4 steps (consolidating).

VariationVary the activity to keep engagement high while practising the same underlying skill.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

Compare each child's progress only to their own Week 1 attempt, never to peers — the range of normal at this skill is wide.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

When a parent worries about pace: "Every child develops these skills at a different rate — what matters is regular, playful practice."

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: attempting a genuinely hard physical skill in front of peers, even imperfectly, is an act of initiative worth naming and celebrating.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Effort Over Mastery

Parents celebrate the attempt at a hard skill, not just a perfect result.

🏠 Try This at Home

Try for five steps in a row without stopping — either counts as real progress.

Communication Pod
Time-Concept Language
▾
Why This Matters

Understanding and using today/tomorrow/yesterday correctly reflects a genuine grasp of time as a sequence, not just an abstract label — a skill that also supports the term's cognitive focus on complex, multi-step pretend play this week.

📚 Book of the Week

If You Give a Mouse a Cookie — Laura Numeroff

A cause-and-effect chain story that supports this week's time-concept language and multi-role pretend play.

Activities
  • Today/tomorrow/yesterday practice (EYLF 5): A simple daily discussion — "what did we do yesterday? What will we do tomorrow?"
  • Spatial concept — away from/toward (EYLF 5): Movement games naming the direction.
  • Body parts consolidation (EYLF 5): A full review of all 23 body parts covered this term, in a game format.

VariationBring the target language into an existing favourite activity rather than treating it as a stand-alone drill.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

Anchor time language to concrete daily events ("yesterday was Play Pods day") rather than abstract calendar concepts, which are still developing.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

When a parent answers for the child: "Try waiting five seconds before stepping in — the pause does more work than a prompt."

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: offering language, even imperfectly, is a small act of communicative initiative — respond warmly to the attempt.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Model, Then Wait

Parents offer the language model, then give real waiting time before jumping in.

Cognitive Pod
Complex Pretend Play, Multiple Roles
Pre-Literacy: Alphabet recitation — first half
▾
Why This Matters

Sustaining a pretend scenario with multiple characters and a loose storyline — not just parallel pretend play — reflects significant cognitive and social growth, and connects directly with this week's book about a chain of consequences.

🧠 Pre-Literacy / Numeracy Threads This Week

Pre-Literacy: Alphabet recitation — first half + Numeracy: Reading numerals to 100

Activities
  • Multi-role pretend scenario (EYLF 4): A shop, a vet clinic, or a cafe with distinct roles (customer, shopkeeper) that children can swap between.
  • Pre-literacy — Alphabet recitation — first half (EYLF 5).
  • Numeracy — Reading numerals to 100 (EYLF 4).

VariationTurn the core activity into a short game with a silly or novel twist to keep motivation high.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

Join the pretend play in a supporting role yourself initially — this models how to sustain a scenario without directing it.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

On persistence: if a child loses interest partway through a tricky cognitive task, invite one more playful attempt rather than letting the first try be the only one.

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: guessing or attempting an answer in front of others carries a small social risk — a relaxed tone protects a child's willingness to keep trying.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Support the Thinking, Not the Answer

Parents make the task slightly easier when needed, rather than supplying the answer directly.

🏠 Try This at Home

A shop or a cafe with a customer and a shopkeeper — swap roles partway through.

Social-Emotional Pod
Recognising a Wider Range of Emotions in Others
SEL: Social Awareness
▾
Why This Matters

Beyond the basic happy/sad/angry/scared set, 4-year-olds are ready to start recognising more nuanced emotions in others — frustrated, proud, disappointed, excited — building genuine emotional vocabulary and social awareness.

Activities
  • Emotion picture sort (EYLF 2): Photos or illustrations of varied facial expressions, naming the more nuanced emotion shown.
  • Story-based emotion spotting (EYLF 2): While reading this week's book, pause and ask how a character might be feeling and why.

VariationLook for a natural moment during free play where this week's skill genuinely comes up, rather than only practising it in a set activity.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

Offer two options rather than an open question at first — "does he look frustrated or proud?" — recognition is easier than free generation.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

Model the target behaviour yourself first — children copy what they see far more readily than what they're told.

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: this term has tracked a steady shift from needing support (Autonomy) toward attempting things independently in a social context (Initiative) — this week's skill sits squarely in that shift.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Notice and Name It

Parents notice and warmly name the target behaviour when it happens, rather than only correcting when it doesn't.

🏠 Try This at Home

Three or four items on a simple checklist, done independently.

Self-Help Pod
Packing Own Bag / Visual Routine
ADL: Routine Independence
▾
Why This Matters

Following a visual routine to pack a bag (water bottle, hat, jacket) independently is a direct rehearsal for the kind of multi-step, self-directed routine school expects — genuinely different from being handed items one at a time.

Activities
  • Visual routine bag-packing (EYLF 3): A picture checklist of 3–4 items, followed independently.
  • Self-check (EYLF 3): Before leaving, ask the child to check their own bag against the picture list.

VariationBuild the practice into a real daily routine rather than a stand-alone drill — the transfer to real life matters most.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

Keep the visual list to 3–4 items at first — more than that overwhelms the working-memory benefit the visual support is meant to provide.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

When a parent steps in too quickly: "Give it a little longer before helping — the attempt itself is the practice."

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: "I can do it myself" is the developmental hallmark of this stage — even when it's slower or messier than an adult stepping in.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Let Them Struggle Before You Help

Parents wait through a genuine struggle before stepping in, rather than helping out of habit.

🏠 Try This at Home

Anchor it to something real — "yesterday was Play Pods day."
