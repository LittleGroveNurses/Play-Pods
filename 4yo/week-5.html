Week 5

Week 5 builds on the term's progression: pedalling a tricycle or scooter in motor, category vocabulary in communication, pattern completion (ab, abc) in cognitive development, understanding and following game rules in social-emotional growth, and independent toileting routine in self-help. This week also continues the balance, locomotor warm-up, and letter/numeral threads running across the whole term.

Gross Motor Pod
Pedalling a Tricycle or Scooter
FMS: Pedalling / Wheeled Locomotion
▾
Why This Matters

Pedalling requires a genuinely different, bilateral coordination pattern from walking or running — alternating leg pushes in a circular motion — and is a common area where 4-year-olds vary widely in prior experience.

🏃 FMS Technique Cues — Pedalling a Tricycle or Scooter
Both hands on the handlebars
Push down, then let the other foot come up to push
Look ahead, not down at your feet
Small, steady pushes — not one big kick

FMS evidence & branding note →

⚖️ Balance Challenge This Week

Stand on the other foot for 5 seconds.

🔄 Locomotor Warm-Up

Tiptoe walking, 4 steps.

Activities
  • Tricycle/scooter practice (EYLF 3): Structured practice time on available wheeled equipment.
  • Pedal motion off the bike (EYLF 3): Practise the alternating leg motion lying on the back, "cycling" legs in the air, if equipment access is limited.
  • Balance challenge (EYLF 3): Stand on the other foot for 5 seconds.
  • Locomotor warm-up (EYLF 3): Tiptoe walking, 4 steps.

VariationVary the activity to keep engagement high while practising the same underlying skill.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

Children with little prior wheeled-toy experience may need several sessions of just sitting and pushing with feet on the ground before pedalling clicks — that's normal, not a delay.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

When a parent worries about pace: "Every child develops these skills at a different rate — what matters is regular, playful practice."

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: attempting a genuinely hard physical skill in front of peers, even imperfectly, is an act of initiative worth naming and celebrating.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Effort Over Mastery

Parents celebrate the attempt at a hard skill, not just a perfect result.

🏠 Try This at Home

If you have a tricycle or scooter, give them regular practice time — it can take a while to click.

Category Vocabulary
▾
Why This Matters

Grouping words into categories (animals, food, vehicles) reflects a more mature, organised vocabulary than simple naming — it's the beginning of the semantic networks that support later reading comprehension.

📚 Book of the Week

Wheels on the Bus — traditional, illus. various

Vehicles and community routines give a natural home for this week's category vocabulary.

Activities
  • Sorting by category (EYLF 5): Picture cards sorted into animals, food, and vehicles, naming the category out loud.
  • Spatial concept — behind/in front of (EYLF 5): Practise positioning objects and describing where.
  • Body parts — chin, tongue, teeth (EYLF 5): Simon Says or a body-part song.

VariationBring the target language into an existing favourite activity rather than treating it as a stand-alone drill.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

If a child sorts inconsistently, ask them to explain their reasoning — sometimes an "incorrect" sort reveals a category they're genuinely reasoning through (e.g. grouping by colour instead).

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

When a parent answers for the child: "Try waiting five seconds before stepping in — the pause does more work than a prompt."

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: offering language, even imperfectly, is a small act of communicative initiative — respond warmly to the attempt.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Model, Then Wait

Parents offer the language model, then give real waiting time before jumping in.

🏠 Try This at Home

Animals, food, vehicles — say the category name out loud together.

Cognitive Pod
Pattern Completion (AB, ABC)
Pre-Literacy: Point to 10 more letters
▾
Why This Matters

Completing and extending simple patterns (red-blue-red-blue…) is foundational mathematical reasoning — recognising and predicting a repeating structure — that later underpins skip-counting and multiplication.

🧠 Pre-Literacy / Numeracy Threads This Week

Pre-Literacy: Point to 10 more letters + Numeracy: Reading numerals to 20

Activities
  • AB pattern completion (EYLF 4): Beads, blocks, or stickers in a simple two-element repeating pattern — what comes next?
  • Pre-literacy — Point to 10 more uppercase letters (EYLF 5).
  • Numeracy — Reading numerals to 20 (EYLF 4).

VariationTurn the core activity into a short game with a silly or novel twist to keep motivation high.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

Start with AB patterns (red-blue-red-blue) before ABC (red-blue-green) — the extra element genuinely increases difficulty.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

On persistence: if a child loses interest partway through a tricky cognitive task, invite one more playful attempt rather than letting the first try be the only one.

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: guessing or attempting an answer in front of others carries a small social risk — a relaxed tone protects a child's willingness to keep trying.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Support the Thinking, Not the Answer

Parents make the task slightly easier when needed, rather than supplying the answer directly.

🏠 Try This at Home

Red-blue-red-blue-red... what comes next? Use blocks, beads, or even socks.

Social-Emotional Pod
Understanding and Following Game Rules
SEL: Relationship Skills
▾
Why This Matters

Rule-based games (rather than free play) ask children to hold a shared, external structure in mind and adjust their own behaviour to it — a step beyond simple turn-taking toward genuine rule-governed cooperative play.

Activities
  • Simple rule game (EYLF 2): A game with 1–2 clear rules, explained before play and reinforced gently during it.
  • "What's the rule?" check-ins (EYLF 2): Pause mid-game and ask a child to state the rule back — checks understanding without singling out a mistake.

VariationLook for a natural moment during free play where this week's skill genuinely comes up, rather than only practising it in a set activity.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

If a child bends the rules to their advantage, that's typical rule-testing at this age, not defiance — restate the rule warmly rather than framing it as trouble.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

Model the target behaviour yourself first — children copy what they see far more readily than what they're told.

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: this term has tracked a steady shift from needing support (Autonomy) toward attempting things independently in a social context (Initiative) — this week's skill sits squarely in that shift.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Notice and Name It

Parents notice and warmly name the target behaviour when it happens, rather than only correcting when it doesn't.

🏠 Try This at Home

Support less each week — clothing, wiping, and handwashing, in order.

Self-Help Pod
Independent Toileting Routine
ADL: Toileting Independence
▾
Why This Matters

By 4, the target is the child recognising and acting on toileting needs largely without being asked, managing clothing, and completing the full routine — not just using the toilet when prompted.

Activities
  • Full routine practice (EYLF 3): Support the whole sequence — clothing, toileting, wiping, hand hygiene — stepping back where the child can manage independently.
  • Visual routine card (EYLF 3): A step-by-step picture sequence as a self-check reference.

VariationBuild the practice into a real daily routine rather than a stand-alone drill — the transfer to real life matters most.

🪜 Scaffolding This Activity

Occasional accidents remain developmentally normal at 4 — respond matter-of-factly rather than with concern, which keeps the child's own confidence intact.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

When a parent steps in too quickly: "Give it a little longer before helping — the attempt itself is the practice."

🌱 Development Note (Erikson)

Initiative vs Guilt: "I can do it myself" is the developmental hallmark of this stage — even when it's slower or messier than an adult stepping in.

👪 Parent Coaching Focus: Let Them Struggle Before You Help

Parents wait through a genuine struggle before stepping in, rather than helping out of habit.

🏠 Try This at Home

Pause and ask "what's the rule?" partway through to check understanding.
