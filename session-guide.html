Session Guide

Facilitator reference — session structure, song bank, snack & reflection prompts, and weekly planning checklist.

🗓️ Session Structure (reconstructed)

A typical Play Pods session follows a consistent structure each week, so children and parents know what to expect:

  1. Opening song — the Hello Song, sung the same way every week as a consistent arrival ritual.
  2. Rotation 1 — small groups move through two pods (e.g. Motor and Communication) matched to the week's focus.
  3. Rotation 2 — groups move to a further pod selected for that week.
  4. Snack & reflection time — see below for the interoception-focused snack routine and rotating parent reflection questions.
  5. Closing song — the Goodbye Song, closing every session with the same predictable ritual.

🗓️ Session Phases

👋 Phase 1 — Opening Songs and Arrival

Begin every session with the Hello Song, sung the same way each week — a predictable ritual that signals the session has started, which matters particularly for children who benefit from routine consistency. As children arrive, this is the first self-help coaching window of the session.

👪 Arrival Dressing

Use a consistent three-step cueing approach for arrival routines — removing shoes and coat, hanging up bags: (1) name the task, (2) point to or demonstrate the first step, (3) wait before helping.

This is the same approach mirrored at departure — see Phase 6, which uses identical three-step cueing for the end-of-session dressing routine.

🔄 Phase 2 — Play Pod Rotation 1 (Two Pods: Motor + Communication)

Two small groups run in parallel through the Motor and Communication pods, matched to that week's specific skill focus. Groups swap partway through the rotation so every child experiences both pods before the session moves on.

🎯 Facilitator Coaching Cues

Motor and Communication are run every week as the two fixed pods in this first rotation — this is also typically the highest-energy part of the session, so keep transitions between the two pods brisk and clearly signalled.

🌿 Phase 3 — Transition and Regulation Break

A short, unstructured break between the first rotation and snack. This buffer matters particularly for 3-year-old groups, who often need co-regulation support after the higher-energy Motor and Communication pods and before the more structured snack and reflection phase.

🪜 Using the Calm Corner

The calm corner and comfort objects area (set up as part of the Weekly Planning Checklist below) are available during this window — facilitators can invite any child who seems overstimulated to spend a minute there before snack begins.

🍎 Phase 4 — Snack & Reflection Time

Opening the snack phase — use every week

"Before we start snack, let's do a body check. [To children] Put your hand on your tummy. Is it saying hungry? A little bit hungry? Really hungry? Let's eat and listen to what it tells us."

"Today at snack, your job is to notice what your child's body is telling them. We're not going for a clean plate — we're going for children who know what their body needs."

Rotating reflective questions — one per week in sequence
Week Question Theme

Snack coaching cues — common parent patterns

One more bite: "His body is saying enough — you've done your job by offering it."

Praising a clean plate: "Over time, we want him to eat to his body, not to our reaction."

Withholding food until another is eaten: "Research suggests this increases resistance to the withheld food. If it's on the table, that's your job done."

Anxiety about intake volume: "Children's appetites are genuinely variable day to day. A small snack today is fine as long as growth is on track."

'I don't like it' without trying: "They don't have to try it. Repeated exposure without pressure is the evidence-based approach."

🪜 Snack Preparation (3-Year-Olds)

Consider having children prepare their own snack — peel a banana, scoop cereal. The preparation is the practice. It takes 3 extra minutes and is worth every second.

🪀 Phase 5 — Play Pod Rotation 2 (One Pod)

One pod after snack — usually Cognitive, Social-Emotional, or Self-Help. Lower energy post-snack makes this ideal for quieter, more focused play. Often the deepest parent coaching window of the session.

🧠 The Silence Game (3-Year-Old Groups)

Dim the lights or ring a small bell: 'Let's see if we can all be completely quiet and still for 30 seconds.' An effective transition tool that simultaneously builds inhibitory control. The facilitator participates alongside the children. Start with 20–30 seconds; extend to 60 seconds over later weeks.

🎵 Phase 6 — Closing Songs and Farewell

The closing ritual mirrors the opening. Always end with the Goodbye Song. After songs, transition immediately to the departure dressing routine — the second self-help coaching window of the session.

👪 Departure Dressing

Use exactly the same three-step cueing approach as arrival. By weeks 5–7, many children will begin to self-initiate dressing at departure.

After dressing: farewell to each child and parent individually. Hand each family the take-home handout: 'Here's one thing to try at home this week.'

🎵 Song Reference

Select 1–2 songs per session that link to the week's session skills. Always include the Hello Song (opening) and Goodbye Song (closing) as fixed ritual anchors.

SongAgePod / SkillWhy It Works
Hello Song (name-based) All ages SE — Belonging Fixed ritual anchor. Peer name recognition, turn-taking, group belonging signal every week.
Hop Little Bunnies 2–3 years Motor: Hopping + IC Hopping FMS with structured stop/go cue. Freeze phase builds inhibitory control.
Five Little Speckled Frogs 2–3 years Motor: Jumping + WM Countdown builds working memory and number sequencing alongside jumping FMS.
Grand Old Duke of York 2–4 years Motor: Running + IC Direction change (up/down) embedded in lyrics — inhibitory control through movement.
Freeze Dance 2–4 years Motor: Locomotion + IC Any locomotion during play; freeze on signal. Primary EF target: inhibitory control.
Old MacDonald (with pause) 1–2 years Communication: OWL Pause before animal sound — 5 second wait. Classic OWL expectant pause at single-word level.
Head Shoulders Knees and Toes 2–4 years Communication + IC + WM Body part vocabulary. Drop a word each round: IC (resist saying it) + WM (hold which is missing).
BINGO (letter drop) 3–4 years Communication: WM Progressive cloze — letter replaced by clap. Phonological working memory. Best for 3–4 year olds.
Boom Chicka Boom 2–4 years SE: Turn-taking Call-and-response models social reciprocity. Facilitator leads, group echoes.
If You're Happy (extended) 2–4 years SE: Emotion naming Extend with sad, angry, scared, surprised verses. Emotion naming + body actions.
Ring Around the Rosie 1–3 years SE: Group cooperation Shared physical experience, proprioceptive regulation, shared IC moment at 'all fall down'.
Goodbye Song All ages SE — Transition ritual Mirror of Hello Song. Fixed ritual. Teaches children that endings are safe.

📚 Facilitator Resources

Hanen Book Nook

Each week's communication pod is supported by a curated book recommendation matched to the communication theme. The Hanen Book Nook provides free, research-based strategies for turning shared book reading into a conversation — reading with the child, not to the child.

Core principle: pause at a predictable point, wait for the child to fill in a word or phrase, and follow their attention before adding language to it. This is OWL in a book-reading context. See each week page for the specific book recommendation.

Book Nook is free public Hanen content — see the Evidence & References page for what Play Pods can/can't say about Hanen-branded programs.
